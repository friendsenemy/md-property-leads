How the sheriff's-sale play works — judgment → lien → writ → levy → sale → ratification
A creditor (usually an HOA or condo) with a money judgment records a Notice of Lien in circuit court, asks the clerk for a writ of execution, and the sheriff levies by posting it on the property. The sheriff can't sell until 30 days after the levy and 20 days after the newspaper notice (Rule 3-644). The buyer gets the owner's interest subject to the mortgage and prior liens; the court then has to ratify the sale (Rule 14-305) — a giveaway price on a home with real equity can be set aside (Baltimore XV v. Newsteps' Choice, 2026).
